# M2 walkthrough: sketch, chart and playback

About 20–30 minutes. Work top to bottom and tick boxes as you go. Anything odd, jot it under **Notes** at the end (a few words is plenty) and hand the list to Claude.

## 0. Set up (2 min)

```sh
cd ~/llm-sandbox/harmonic_atlas_v2
git checkout m2
npm install        # M2 adds the `tonal` package
npm run dev
```

- [ ] Open the printed URL (usually <http://localhost:5173>) in **Firefox**. Turn the volume to a sensible level.
- [ ] Press `` ` `` (backtick, left of 1) for the frame-time overlay in the bottom-left corner. It should stay green. The budget is all views together ≤ 8 ms per frame.

The screen, top to bottom: top bar (play, tempo, meter, loop, click, count-in, sketch name, sound, voicing mode, undo/redo, Chart), then the Tonnetz (left) beside the readout and sketch list (right), then the timeline, with a small piano strip under it.

## 1. The big test: paste, loop, drift, undo, reload (10 min)

This is the M2 done-when.

1. Press `C` to open the chart drawer on the right.
2. Select all the text in the drawer and replace it with the contents of [m2-sample-chart.txt](m2-sample-chart.txt) (open the file in an editor, copy everything, paste).
   - [ ] The drawer header reads **32 bars · 58 chords**, and the timeline fills with blocks.
3. Click on the Tonnetz (so you're not typing in the drawer) and press **Space**.
   - [ ] You hear chords. The green playhead moves along the timeline, the timeline pages along, and the playing block brightens.
   - [ ] The piano strip shows the playing voicing (green = bass, amber = the rest).
   - [ ] After bar 32 it loops back to bar 1.
4. Watch the Tonnetz while it plays.
   - [ ] A trail: faint shapes for the last 8 chords, joined centroid to centroid with small arrowheads. Amber = a single P/L/R flip, blue = a 2–3 step compound move, dashed grey = a bigger jump.
   - [ ] A green ring on the bass note. The readout says **Playing** and the chord name (e.g. `A7`).
   - [ ] With **Follow** on (top bar), the view keeps the playing chord on screen.
   - [ ] The overlay stays green (≤ 8 ms) during playback.
5. **The drift question (please decide).** As it plays, notice two things:
   - Each I–vi–ii–V–I loop shifts the trail sideways by one lap of the lattice. It's the same spelled chords, just further left each time.
   - At Fmaj7 → Fm7 → Em7 (bars 6–7), the spellings flip to flats: Em7 shows as F♭m7, and later Am7 as B♭♭m7 (with `=` hints where it gets extreme).

   This is the spec's rule ("nearest copy on screen") working as written. **Do you like it, or should the trail prefer to keep one spelling unless another copy is clearly closer?** Note your answer under Notes.
6. Press **Space** to stop. Now undo:
   - [ ] **Ctrl+Z** repeatedly walks back each edit, one step at a time. The whole paste is a single step, so one Ctrl+Z after the paste empties the timeline. **Ctrl+Shift+Z** brings it back.
   - [ ] Undo never moves the camera.
7. Reload the page (**F5**).
   - [ ] The same sketch comes back: chords, tempo 112, name, camera position, and the trail in the same place.

## 2. Chart drawer details (5 min)

Open the drawer with `C` if it's closed (Esc or × closes it).

- [ ] Change one chord to nonsense, e.g. `Em7` → `Xq`. It gets a red wavy underline, an error appears below, and if you press Space the last valid version still plays. Fix it and the timeline updates immediately.
- [ ] Try a few on a new line at the end:
  - `| C/E . F G |` → E in the bass (green ring, lowest piano key)
  - `| C >P >L >P |` → C, Cm, A♭, A♭m
  - `| [0 4 7 11] | {C E G B} | _ | % |` → numbers, note names, a rest, a repeated bar
- [ ] Change `@tempo 112` to `@tempo 140` and the tempo box follows. Change the tempo box (type a number, press Enter) and only the `@tempo` line changes in the text.
- [ ] Open **Syntax** at the bottom of the drawer and check the cheat sheet reads clearly.

## 3. Building on the Tonnetz (5 min)

Start fresh: **+ New** in the Sketches list (right column).

- [ ] Click a triangle (you hear it), then press **Enter**. A one-bar block appears in the timeline, and the amber caret (insert point) moves after it.
- [ ] Press **Shift+P**, **Shift+L**, **Shift+P**, **Shift+L**…: each one transforms *and* appends. The Tonnetz trail draws a straight strip.
- [ ] **Shift-click** a triangle to append it directly.
- [ ] Press **←** twice to move the caret back two chords, then **Enter**. The new chord goes in there and later chords shift right.
- [ ] Click a timeline block: you hear its voicing, it lights up on the Tonnetz with its name in the readout, and **P/L/R** then continue from it.
- [ ] With a block selected, **Backspace** deletes it and closes the gap.
- [ ] Rename the sketch in the top bar. It shows in the Sketches list. Click between two sketches and each reopens with its own chords and camera.

## 4. Transport and sound (5 min)

- [ ] **Loop:** drag across the numbers in the timeline ruler (the thin strip at the top). It snaps to bars, or hold Shift for beats. The top bar shows `Loop a–b`, and Space plays just that region. Click the ruler once to clear it.
- [ ] Change the loop while playing. If the playhead ends up outside it, it jumps to the new start.
- [ ] **Click** = metronome (accent on the downbeat). **Count-in** = one bar of clicks before playback.
- [ ] Switch the sound (E-piano / Pad / Pluck) while playing.
- [ ] Switch the voicing (smooth / close / drop2 / spread) and watch the piano strip change. In *smooth*, a repeated chord's keys don't move.
- [ ] Click Tonnetz triangles while playing. The audition plays on top without cutting playback off.
- [ ] Edit while playing (delete a chord, change tempo, type in the chart). Playback carries on with the change.
